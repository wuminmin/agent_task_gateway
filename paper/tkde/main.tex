Versioned Reporting Snapshots}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)%
\thanks{The artifact includes a controlled local PostgreSQL performance
campaign and a three-scale TPC-H-derived Join--Group campaign. Results remain
single-host evidence; the latter is not an official TPC-H run and neither
campaign claims production generality.}}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
We study enterprise analytical agents that query governed reporting replicas
refreshed through scheduled daily synchronization rather than mutable OLTP
primaries. Each cut becomes an immutable, versioned Reporting Snapshot; every
approved root and delegated child remains bound to one publication for life.
Within that epoch, adaptive rewrites, pages,
retries, and delegation can accumulate disclosure. TaskGate meters this
accumulation with typed FactID sets for released values, conservative
positive-output dependencies, and successful query outcomes. Before release,
the visible query and provenance companion observe one PostgreSQL snapshot;
TaskGate derives exact effects and atomically charges only facts novel to the
root-family ledger. Semantic replay is free of new exposure only within the
same publication-bound identity. We give semantics for a positive relational
fragment, conservation properties, and finite-state checks. A legacy
materialized ledger exposes million-fact cost, motivating V4: immutable
publication-time ordinals, streamed compressed bitmaps, and sparse dynamic
dictionaries. Independent-oracle, rewrite, concurrency, PostgreSQL, and
fixed-host V4 experiments validate the scoped mechanisms. Four \RQFiveRows{}-row daily
publications finish every measured cycle within
\RQFiveMaxCycleS{}~s, and a retained-route campaign passes all five rollover conditions.
TaskGate targets periodic reporting publications; mutable OLTP, continuous
CDC, intra-task epoch switching, and cross-publication knowledge accounting
are outside scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{D}{atabase} authorization answers whether a principal may execute
a particular operation. An adaptive client creates a second problem: during a
single human-approved task it can adaptively issue many individually legal
queries. It can split a projection, overlap pages, retry with a new request
identifier, ask an equivalent query, or delegate a narrower subtask. Per-query
authorization and row limits do not state how much distinct data the resulting
root ledger has already accounted. This is not a model of the agent's knowledge.

Counting queries is insensitive to answer size. Counting returned rows makes a
one-row aggregate appear cheap even when it depends on millions of source rows.
Counting bytes or cells varies with encoding and query shape. Monetary query
pricing has developed formal anti-arbitrage objectives
\cite{koutris2012query,deep2017qirana,peng2025aria}, while provenance explains
where results came from \cite{green2007provenance,amsterdamer2011aggregate}.
Our question is narrower and operational: how can a human authorization create
a stateful, explicit-disclosure budget that remains conserved while adaptive
clients query and delegate? The mechanism neither inspects an LLM nor depends
on an agent-specific planning algorithm; agents are one important client class.

Enterprise operational analytics commonly separates transactional systems
from reporting access. Production systems feed a governed analytical replica
through scheduled ETL or synchronization; TaskGate does not query the OLTP
primary. After each cut, TaskGate compiles the resulting dataset into an
immutable publication. For example, an overnight synchronization may finish at
01:00, after which that day's approved tasks bind to the new publication. The
next cut creates another publication for newly approved tasks, while any
unfinished task and all its descendants continue reading the prior one in a
retained, version-routed deployment; without that route, access fails closed.
Thus TaskGate addresses adaptive disclosure within one approved reporting
epoch, not freshness or consistency over a continuously changing database.

\system treats the root task as the budget subject. A result has three effects.
\emph{Release exposure} identifies values actually delivered.
\emph{Positive-output dependency} is a contract-defined conservative footprint
of base facts participating in the successful derivation of delivered output.
It is neither minimal causal provenance nor the complete physical read set.
This second dimension prevents a count from being accounted as one input fact
merely because it returns one cell. Both effects are sets, so a fact already
recorded in the root-family ledger has zero incremental charge.  The third,
\emph{query outcome}, binds the normalized predicate and relational context to
the released-result digest and visible cardinality. Thus ``no row satisfies
$x>100$'' and ``no row satisfies $x>200$'' consume two outcome facts even when
their release and dependency sets coincide; an exact replay remains free.

The system follows a withhold-before-settlement discipline:

\begin{center}
\small
\code{reserve -> replay lookup / execute+stream -> derive -> settle -> release}.
\end{center}

The visible query and its ordinal provenance companion observe one frozen
publication in a PostgreSQL snapshot. The gateway releases neither until a
control-database transaction has computed exact bitmap differences, advanced
one three-dimensional root head, checked all Catalog limits, settled resource
use, stored the encrypted result, and persisted audit and receipt evidence. A
failed limit check rolls the transaction back while the business execution
remains separately measurable. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture-settlement} separates
the offline publication index from the online withhold-before-settlement path.

A preliminary preprint version introduced the task-scoped authorization
substrate \cite{wu2026sessionbound}: approval-bound short-lived database
sessions, signed task tokens, SQL/view/scope enforcement, resource budgets,
and audit receipts.\footnote{Preliminary version: arXiv:2607.00751v1.} The
present, substantially revised article develops the database-specific
accounting semantics, implementation, formal analysis, and evaluation. Its
five implementation-backed contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A versioned, typed FactID and three-set semantics separating delivered
release facts, positive-output dependencies, and normalized query outcomes in
a restricted relational algebra.
\item A same-snapshot streamed ordinal companion for one-product plans and
bounded two-scan inner equijoins/union-distinct, including grouping and four
aggregates.
\item A representation-independent exact settlement rule, physically refined
by immutable snapshot dictionaries, segmented compressed bitmaps, and sparse
dynamic dictionaries: atomically charge $E_q\setminus\accounted_T$, withhold on
any limit, and make semantic replay zero-novelty for exposure (ordinary
query/row resource accounting still applies).
\item Signed, monotonic root-family delegation whose descendants share one
three-dimensional ledger, preventing task-tree fan-out from multiplying exposure capacity.
\item New formal properties and evidence for these mechanisms: three-budget
safety, exact/non-overlapping novel charge, no pre-settlement delivery, and
task-family non-amplification; algebraic/rewrite tests, a finite TLA+ ledger
model, and full-path PostgreSQL experiments through one million dependency
facts.
\end{enumerate}

The implementation does \emph{not} claim arbitrary SQL equivalence, differential
privacy, inference control, principal-wide accounting, or denial-oblivious
auditing. The online multi-input grammar is intentionally bounded: two scan
leaves, an inner conjunctive equijoin or same-product union-distinct, and no
nested set/join tree or multi-input pagination. A newly approved root creates a
distinct ledger. Outcome accounting bounds the number of distinct successful
propositions admitted by this grammar; it is not an information-theoretic
bound on the bits revealed by one proposition. These boundaries are part of the result, rather than hidden
behind a broad ``SQL support'' label.

The commercial fit is a governed analytics or operations copilot operating
inside one approved case: finance agents testing reimbursement/credit
thresholds, fraud or compliance investigators refining ``no matching case''
queries, customer-support agents searching entitlement conditions, and
delegated analysts splitting one investigation. Release limits cap repeated
value extraction, dependency limits expose large-source aggregates, and the
outcome limit makes adaptive yes/no or empty-result probing consume explicit
case capacity. Organizations that need population-level privacy across many
approved roots still require differential privacy or a tenant-wide policy;
TaskGate supplies auditable workflow conservation, not that replacement.

\cite{stanley2026gaap}. It keeps private data and permissions outside the

\subsection{History-Based Query Auditing and Inference Control}

History-based auditing treats a query log, not one query, as the security
object. Hippocratic auditing reconstructs access \cite{agrawal2004hippocratic};
batch auditing asks whether answers determine a forbidden view
\cite{motwani2008auditing}; simulatable auditing protects the accept/deny bit
\cite{kenthapadi2005simulatable}; and inference-control surveys include prior
knowledge \cite{farkas2002inference}. TaskGate neither models such knowledge nor
protects denial leakage or implicit inference. It charges a successful empty or
zero answer as one normalized proposition, not an estimate of its information,
and scopes history to one signed root family. Stronger history controls remain
complementary.

\subsection{Query Pricing and Privacy Accounting}

Query pricing studies determinacy and arbitrage \cite{koutris2012query}; QIRANA
extends history-aware pricing to broad SQL with aggregation
\cite{deep2017qirana}, and ARIA uses tuple information gain for SPJA and simple
aggregates \cite{peng2025aria}. Weighting, historical charging, and rewrite
resistance are therefore established. TaskGate instead admits against explicit
contract facts, scopes state to a root family, and withholds delivery when
atomic settlement fails.

CacheDP reuses noisy answers \cite{mazmudar2022cachedp}, while DProvDB tracks
privacy loss by analyst provenance \cite{zhang2023dprovdb}; both compose
randomized mechanisms \cite{dwork2014foundations}. TaskGate releases exact
answers, offers no $\epsilon$-DP guarantee, and bounds only facts recognized by
its active profile.

\subsection{Provenance Foundations and Systems}

Why- and where-provenance identify contributing data and output locations
\cite{buneman2001why}, while lineage traces warehouse outputs back through
transformations \cite{cui2001lineage}; Cheney et al. survey the resulting
database-provenance design space \cite{cheney2009provenance}. Semiring
provenance provides compositional annotations for positive relational queries
\cite{green2007provenance}, and aggregation requires value-level treatment
beyond tuple annotations \cite{amsterdamer2011aggregate}.

GProM rewrites provenance questions \cite{arab2018gprom}, and ProvSQL adds
general circuit-based provenance and probability to PostgreSQL, including
multiset relational algebra and terminal aggregation
\cite{senellart2018provsql,sen2026provsql}. TaskGate
claims neither a new algebra nor a general provenance engine. It specializes
provenance to explicitly released facts and a conservative positive-derivation
footprint, co-generates answer and effects in one snapshot, and settles before
release. Its profile bounds inner equijoin and union-distinct multiplicity;
outer joins, negation, and recursion remain outside it.

\subsection{Usage Control, Delegation, and Quotas}

Usage control extends authorization across an ongoing use \cite{park2004ucon};
execution-history access control makes past actions part of authorization
\cite{abadi2003history}; and delegation has mature logical foundations
\cite{li2003delegation}. PERMIS combines delegated authorization with
history-sensitive cumulative-resource decisions \cite{chadwick2008permis}.

These works establish attenuation, delegation depth, consumable authority, and
quotas. TaskGate's narrower claim is that every descendant settles its semantic
effect against one root ledger, so delegation cannot mint disclosure capacity.
The prototype is centralized; distributed vouchers would need another safety
argument.

\subsection{Database Policy Enforcement and Agents}

Database-backed policy systems rewrite or validate queries against application
policy \cite{mehta2017qapla,zhang2022blockaid}. Recent work aligns
natural-language interfaces with database policy \cite{liu2026safenlidb},
measures indirect prompt injection against tool-using agents
\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo}, and applies runtime data-flow control
\cite{summers2026dataflow}.

These systems target per-request policy, prompt-injection resilience, or
information-flow labels. TaskGate likewise treats generated SQL and client
tools as untrusted, but focuses on cumulative explicit database disclosure
across retries, pages, supported rewrites, and delegation.

\textbf{Novelty boundary.} The literature establishes history, inference
control, pricing, provenance, mediation, delegation, quotas, task-bound SQL,
receipts, and persistent disclosure logs. Within this boundary, the present
article develops three-set FactID semantics, same-snapshot provenance, exact
novel-set settlement, and shared-root delegation, together with formal and
empirical checks. Relative to GAAP, it is database-specific semantic accounting
rather than cross-task IFC: narrower than noninterference, but stronger than
stateless authorization for the modeled threat.

\section{Exposure Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Publication Epochs and Task Binding}

TaskGate's upstream boundary is a completed reporting synchronization, not a
mutable transactional database. We model its compiled output as follows.

\begin{definition}[Publication epoch]
A publication epoch is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 P_e=\langle& datasource,schema,cutoff,\\
             & snapshot,catalog,dictionary\rangle,
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:publication-epoch}
\end{equation}
where \emph{cutoff} is the completed synchronization watermark,
\emph{snapshot} is the immutable reporting dataset, \emph{catalog} binds its
schema, policy, and lineage, and \emph{dictionary} is the set of V4 ordinal
dictionaries. We write $h_e$ for the digest of a canonical descriptor of all
six components. This is a model-level epoch identity: in the prototype the
signed Catalog SHA transitively commits the datasource/schema, snapshot ID,
and manifest/dictionary/sidecar digests, while the synchronization cutoff is
co-versioned deployment metadata rather than a field in the ordinal manifest.
A new synchronization creates a new $P_{e+1}$; it never mutates $P_e$.
\end{definition}

Any deployment alias such as \code{latest publication} must resolve to the
concrete Catalog digest whose transitive binding instantiates $P_e$ in the
authorization snapshot presented for approval; that binding becomes final
when approval succeeds. The prototype
has no mutable runtime alias: it eagerly writes the candidate digest into the
draft and approval revalidates it, so a deployer that requires the newest epoch
after a rollover must cancel and reissue the draft rather than rewrite it. In
particular, \emph{``latest'' is resolved at approval time, never at query time}. Let
$\mathsf Q(T)$ contain every query issued by an approved root
$T$ or any delegated descendant. Then
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{publication}(T)&=P_e,\\
 \forall q\in\mathsf Q(T),\quad \operatorname{publication}(q)&=P_e.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:publication-binding}
\end{equation}
The Catalog transitively binds the grant and FactID environment to that epoch;
ledger state, replay metadata, result evidence, audit events, and V6 receipts
carry the corresponding publication and dictionary bindings. Execution,
settlement, and replay fail closed if they differ. Consequently an active root
cannot switch publications, even after the next cut becomes active for new
approvals.

\subsection{Facts and Task State}

The V2 release/dependency algebra treats a FactID as a semantic object and its SHA-256 as only a database
index.  Its semantic environment is
$\Pi=\langle pg,\mathcal T,UTC,\mathcal C,D,h_C\rangle$: a PostgreSQL major
version, admitted type domain, timezone, exact deterministic collation
name/version map, immutable snapshot map, and catalog/lineage digest.  A
judgment is defined only for an \emph{admissible} $\Pi$: source schemas and
entity keys are attested, every collatable field has a deterministic collation,
and volatile expressions are absent.  Let $\nu$ be a catalog-owned source
namespace (not a view alias), $s$ an immutable snapshot, $e$ a canonical stable
entity key, $a$ a role-qualified field ID, $\tau\in\mathcal T$, and
$C_\tau(v)$ its typed canonical value.  Base identities are

\begin{align}
 f_{row}&=\langle\pi,\nu,s,e\rangle,\\
 f_{cell}&=\langle\pi,\nu,s,e,a,\tau,C_\tau(v)\rangle .
 \label{eq:fact}
\end{align}

A derived identity is
$f_d=\langle\pi,\mathcal B,k_o,N_\pi(x),\tau,C_\tau(v),CW(W)\rangle$,
where $\mathcal B=\operatorname{sort}\{(\nu,s)\}$ is the relation's snapshot
bundle, $k_o$ is the canonical output-row entity key, $N_\pi(x)$ erases aliases
and normalizes the supported expression, and
$W:\facts_{base}\rightharpoonup\mathbb N^+$
is a finite-support witness multiset (the empty support is allowed).  The product identity is not a caller-supplied
name: it is exactly the namespace projection of $\mathcal B$.  The witness
commitment $CW(W)$ length-prefixes the lexically sorted
$(\operatorname{FactHash}(f),W(f))$ pairs; the empty multiset has a separate
token.  The durable key is
$\mathrm{SHA256}(\texttt{TASKGATE-FACT-V2\textbackslash0}\|\operatorname{Canon}(f))$.
Control PostgreSQL stores $\operatorname{Canon}(f)$ and compares it on every hash conflict;
a same-hash, different-payload row aborts the transaction. V1 and V2 tagged
payloads are disjoint. V3 preserves these V2 identities and adds an outcome
kind under a disjoint hash domain.

Let $N(q)$ be the accepted query normal form, $h_N$ its SHA-256, $m$ the
visible row count, and
$h_R=H(m,\operatorname{sort}\{\operatorname{Canon}(f):f\in\facts_r\})$. Let
$d_3=\texttt{TASKGATE-FACT-V3\textbackslash0}$ be the outcome hash domain.
V3 creates exactly one successful-outcome identity
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 f_o&=\langle\pi_3,\operatorname{ver}(N),h_N,h_R,m\rangle,\\
 \operatorname{FactHash}(f_o)&=H(d_3\|\operatorname{Canon}(f_o)).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:outcome-fact}
\end{equation}
The normal form contains the Catalog namespace, snapshot/lineage binding,
typed predicates, grouping, ordering, pagination, null/bag semantics,
collations, timezone, and exact numeric mode. It erases only the explicitly
supported aliases and reorderings. Therefore the proposition is server-bound,
not caller-named; the result commitment is explicit even for $m=0$ or a
one-row scalar zero.

\begin{theorem}[Fact identity well-definedness]
\label{thm:fact-identity}
For every value in the V2 supported PostgreSQL type domain, canonical encoding
is deterministic and injective over the typed semantic payload (that is, over
PostgreSQL semantic-value equivalence, not raw float NaN bit patterns).
\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof in the supplement.}

\begin{definition}[Exposure effect]
For query $q$ over snapshot $D_s$, the V3 effect
$\effect(q,D_s)=\langle \facts_r,\facts_i,\facts_o\rangle$ contains release facts
$\facts_r$ and positive-output dependency facts $\facts_i$ under a versioned
profile $\pi$.  The latter are the conservative base facts selected by the
inductive rules below for successfully derived delivered rows and cells; they
are not claimed to be minimal causal provenance or all physical reads.
$\facts_o=\{f_o\}$ contains the successful proposition/result identity from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:outcome-fact}.
\end{definition}

Every root task $T$ stores previously accounted sets
$\accounted_T=\langle\accounted_T^r,\accounted_T^i,\accounted_T^o\rangle$ and limits
$B_T=\langle B_T^r,B_T^i,B_T^o\rangle$. Settlement computes

\begin{align}
 \Delta_T^r(q) &= \facts_r(q,D_s)\setminus\accounted_T^r,\\
 \Delta_T^i(q) &= \facts_i(q,D_s)\setminus\accounted_T^i,\\
 \Delta_T^o(q) &= \facts_o(q,D_s)\setminus\accounted_T^o.
 \label{eq:delta}
\end{align}

V3 measures cardinality independently in each dimension. A query may settle
only if
$|\accounted_T^r|+|\Delta_T^r|\leq B_T^r$ and
$|\accounted_T^i|+|\Delta_T^i|\leq B_T^i$ and
$|\accounted_T^o|+|\Delta_T^o|\leq B_T^o$.

The word ``accounted'' is intentional. Release facts describe delivered values,
but dependency FactIDs remain internal to the gateway; the agent receives
only result data, counts, and receipt digests. Hence $\accounted_T^i$ is not a
set of facts known by the agent, and no component is a complete epistemic
or privacy-loss state.  The wire and storage label \code{influence} is retained
for compatibility; under the V2/V3 profiles it denotes positive-output dependency,
not causal influence.

\begin{theorem}[Outcome separation and replay]
\label{thm:outcome-separation}
For successful queries over the same snapshot, unequal normal-form digests
produce unequal outcome FactIDs even when their released results are equal.
Equal normal forms and equal released FactSets/cardinalities produce the same
outcome FactID. Hence different zero/empty threshold propositions each incur
one novel outcome charge, while exact replay and supported equivalent rewrites
incur zero incremental outcome charge.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof in the supplement.}

\subsection{Closed Typed Query Language}

The complete core syntax is
\begin{align*}
e ::= {}& \operatorname{Scan}[\nu,s,r,\Gamma]
 \mid \sigma_{p;F}(e) \mid \pi_A(e) \mid e\bowtie_{\Theta}e \\
 &\mid e\cup_\delta e \mid \gamma_{G;\mathcal A}(e)
 \mid \operatorname{Page}_{O,K,o,l}(e),\\
p ::= {}& b \mid p\wedge b,\qquad b ::= a\;op\;c,\\
op\in {}&\{=,\ne,<,\le,>,\ge\}
 \cup\{\code{LIKE},\code{IN},\code{NOT IN}\},
\end{align*}
where $F$ is exactly the fields read by $p$, $\Theta$ is a nonempty
conjunction of equalities, $\cup_\delta$ is union-distinct, and
$\mathcal A\subseteq\{\code{COUNT(*)},\code{COUNT},\code{SUM},\code{MIN},
\code{MAX}\}$.  No implicit SQL construct is hidden in $e$: outer joins,
difference, negation beyond the listed scalar predicates, windows, recursion,
subqueries, volatile functions, and \code{UNION ALL} are not terms.

The typing judgment $\Pi\vdash e:\Gamma$ is partial: every operator requires a
typed child; predicates and projections may name only child fields; join
equalities require disjoint role-qualified schemas and identical canonical
types/collations; union branches require one identical distinctable schema;
grouping requires typed group keys and aggregates; and pagination requires a
proven total order. Undefined premises reject the plan. The complete inference
rules are provided in the supplementary material.

Here $\mathbb B_3=\{\mathsf T,\mathsf F,\mathsf U\}$ is PostgreSQL
three-valued logic.  Equality with either operand NULL is $\mathsf U$;
$\mathsf F\wedge x=\mathsf F$, $\mathsf T\wedge x=x$, and
$\mathsf U\wedge\mathsf U=\mathsf U$.  Selection retains only $\mathsf T$.
By contrast, grouping and distinct use the typed equivalence
$v\equiv_\tau w$ defined by \code{IS NOT DISTINCT FROM}, so NULL is equivalent
to NULL.  Predicate evaluation and ordering are PostgreSQL functions fixed by
$pg$ and $\mathcal C$, not Go approximations.

The closed scalar domain $\mathcal T$ contains exactly the canonical types
\code{smallint}, \code{integer}, \code{bigint}, \code{numeric}, \code{real},
\code{double precision}, \code{boolean}, \code{bytea}, \code{date},
\code{time without time zone}, \code{timestamp without time zone},
\code{timestamp with time zone}, \code{uuid}, \code{text}, \code{character},
\code{character varying}, and \code{jsonb}, with PostgreSQL
aliases mapped to these canonical names.  Integers retain their
16/32/64-bit ranges; exact numerics are encoded as reduced rationals and never
accepted through a binary-float conversion; floats use their IEEE width and
canonicalize NaN, infinities, and negative zero.  Temporal values have
PostgreSQL microsecond precision, \code{timestamptz} is UTC, and
\code{time with time zone} is excluded.  Text equality/order binds exact
deterministic collation name and actual version.  The text-preserving
\code{json} type is excluded: PostgreSQL retains insignificant whitespace,
object-key order, duplicate keys, and numeric lexical forms, while \code{jsonb}
has structural canonical equality \cite{postgres16json}.  Aggregate result
types are exactly PostgreSQL's:
\code{COUNT}$\to$\code{int8}, \code{SUM(int2/int4)}$\to$\code{int8},
\code{SUM(int8)}$\to$\code{numeric}, \code{SUM(numeric)}$\to$\code{numeric},
and \code{MIN/MAX}$\to$the input type.  \code{SUM(real)} and
\code{SUM(double precision)} are not admitted: IEEE-754 addition is
non-associative, so a floating-point SUM depends on the physical aggregation
order, which the typed bag and the normal form do not fix.  Any cast,
arithmetic, aggregate, witness-multiplicity, or canonical-range overflow makes
evaluation undefined and the gateway releases and settles nothing.

\subsection{Annotated Bags and Complete Output Keys}

A relation is $R=\langle\Gamma,\mathcal B,M,\prec?\rangle$, where $M$ is a
finite PostgreSQL bag implemented by unique row keys.  Equal value tuples remain
distinct bag occurrences when their keys differ; only union-distinct and group
quotient occurrences into classes.  An optional $\prec$ records a proven total
order.  A row is
$t=\langle k,\{a\mapsto c_a\},S_r,W_r,O_r\rangle$ and a cell is
$c_a=\langle v,\tau,S_a,W_a,x_a,\rho_a?\rangle$.  $S$ is a finite set of
snapshot-covered base facts, $W$ a finite multiset with positive stored
multiplicities (possibly empty), $x$ a canonical
data-independent lineage expression, and $\rho$ an optional already
materialized release fact.  Every operator preserves
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{support}(W_r)=S_r,\qquad
 \operatorname{support}(W_a)=S_a.                 \label{eq:anninv}
\end{equation}
For annotations define same-proof composition
$\bigoplus_i(S_i,W_i)=(\bigcup_iS_i,\sum_iW_i)$ and alternative-proof
composition
$\bigsqcup_i(S_i,W_i)=(\bigcup_iS_i,\max_iW_i)$, with pointwise multiset
sum/max.  Thus $A\bigsqcup A=A$, while join fanout remains in $\bigoplus$.

Let
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{RID}(R,t)=H(\code{relation-row},N(\Gamma),\mathcal B,t.k).
 \label{eq:rid}
\end{equation}
$H$ is domain-separated, typed, and length delimited. Scan uses the Catalog
entity key; unary operators preserve the child key; join hashes the sorted
immediate-child RIDs; union-distinct hashes the complete typed tuple; and group
hashes its canonical group key (or a distinct global token). Every derived Fact
also binds the merged source/snapshot bundle, expression, value, and witness.
Thus equal-looking results from different sources or derivations remain
distinct, while SQL aliases and caller-provided product names do not
participate. The complete output-key construction is in the supplement; a
same-hash unequal key is a validation error, not a row merge.

\subsection{Operator Evaluation and Effect Extraction}

Evaluation is the partial judgment $\Pi;D\vdash e\Downarrow R$; ``partial''
means that a failed premise yields no relation, effect, settlement, or release.
For compactness, $\alpha_r(t)=(S_r,W_r)$,
$\alpha_a(t)=(S_a,W_a)$, $\operatorname{aug}_F(t)$ replaces
$\alpha_r(t)$ by $\alpha_r(t)\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in F}\alpha_a(t)$, and cell
maps are otherwise copied.  In the derived rules a cell is written as the
5-tuple $c_a=\langle v_a,\tau_a,\alpha_a,x_a,\rho_a\rangle$: value, type, the
annotation pair $\alpha_a=(S_a,W_a)$ that expands to support and witness, the
normalized lineage expression, and the optional materialized release fact.
E-Scan alone displays the fully expanded base-cell 6-tuple; every other rule
constructs each output cell explicitly, so representation closure does not rely
on prose. Table~\ref{tab:effect-summary} retains the review-relevant accounting
choices; the complete inference rules, auxiliary functions, NULL/empty-input
cases, and page bounds are in the supplementary material.

Union is conservatively bag-aware and becomes idempotent only when its complete
typed tuples are proven duplicate-free. Grouping/distinct equivalence is
\code{IS NOT DISTINCT FROM}. Exact/integer \code{SUM} is admitted, but
floating-point \code{SUM} is rejected because the typed bag does not fix a
physical fold order. Pagination appends Catalog keys until the PostgreSQL order
is total. These restrictions make the summarized relation a partial function,
not a semantics for arbitrary SQL.

Finally, define materialization and the effect---they are not an implementation
afterthought:
\begin{align}
\operatorname{new}(R,t,a)&=
 f_d(\mathcal B,t.k,N(x_a),\tau_a,v_a,W_a),\\
\operatorname{mat}(R,t,a)&=
\begin{cases}
\rho_a,&\operatorname{valid}(\rho_a,R,t,a),\\
\operatorname{new}(R,t,a),&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}\\
\facts_r(R,V)&=\{\operatorname{mat}(R,t,a)\mid t\in R,\ a\in V\},\\
\facts_i(R,V)&=\bigcup_{t\in R}S_r(t)\ \cup
                \bigcup_{t\in R,a\in V}S_a(t),\\
\rieffect_\Pi(e,D,V)&=\operatorname{normalize}
  \langle\facts_r(R,V),\facts_i(R,V)\rangle.       \label{eq:observe}
\end{align}
Here $\Pi;D\vdash e\Downarrow R$, and $\operatorname{valid}$ checks value,
type and bundle, plus support for a base fact or expression/witness for a
derived fact, against the pre-existing release fact.
Thus query bags and witness multiplicity are retained during composition, while
the two base ledgers are sets.  Equation~\eqref{eq:observe} is the declared
positive-output dependency profile: it is exact with respect to these
inductive rules, not with respect to arbitrary causal provenance or physical
source access.  Empty answers have an empty base effect except for the single
visible row of a global aggregate; V3 nevertheless adds one outcome fact.

\begin{theorem}[Representation closure and partial-function semantics]
\label{thm:representation-closure}
For every admissible $\Pi$, typed finite $e$, and immutable $D$, evaluation has
at most one result.  If $\Pi;D\vdash e\Downarrow R$, $R$ satisfies
Eq.~\eqref{eq:anninv}, has canonical schema/bundle and unique row keys, and is a
valid input to every well-typed parent rule.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof in the supplement.}

\begin{theorem}[Dependency determinism]
\label{thm:dependency-determinism}
Fixed admissible $\Pi$, typed $e$, snapshot map $D$, and visible field set $V$
uniquely determine the positive-output dependency FactSet $\facts_i$, and hence
$\rieffect_\Pi(e,D,V)$, whenever evaluation succeeds.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof in the supplement.}

\begin{property}[Group-key completeness]
\label{prop:group-key-completeness}
For every delivered group $Q$, member $t\in Q$, and group field $g\in G$,
$S_g(t)\subseteq\facts_i$, including when $g\notin V$.
\end{property}
\emph{Proof in the supplement.}

\begin{property}[Distinct-dependency completeness]
\label{prop:distinct-completeness}
For every delivered union-distinct class $Q$, candidate $t\in Q$, and schema
field $a\in\Gamma$, $S_a(t)\subseteq\facts_i$, including when $a\notin V$.
\end{property}
\emph{Proof in the supplement.}

\begin{property}[Conservative aggregate input]
\label{prop:aggregate-input}
Every delivered aggregate cell includes its complete declared logical input:
all member row annotations for \code{COUNT(*)}, and every member argument cell
for \code{COUNT(a)}, \code{SUM(a)}, \code{MIN(a)}, and \code{MAX(a)}.  NULL and
non-extreme \code{MIN/MAX} inputs are not omitted.
\end{property}
\emph{Proof in the supplement.}

\subsection{Conservation Properties}

The following properties apply only to the operators and rewrites stated here.
They are not claims about all semantically equivalent SQL.

\begin{property}[Normalization]
\label{prop:normalization}
Fact-set normalization is deterministic, idempotent, and commutative under
merge.
\end{property}
\emph{Proof in the supplement.}

\begin{theorem}[Restricted projection/selection invariance]
\label{thm:projection-selection}
For $F\cup V\subseteq A$, selection followed by projection and projection
followed by selection have the same visible effect:
\[
 \rieffect_\Pi(\pi_A(\sigma_{p;F}e),D,V)=
 \rieffect_\Pi(\sigma_{p;F}(\pi_Ae),D,V).
\]
Reordering $A$ also preserves this normalized terminal effect.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof in the supplement.}

\begin{definition}[Typed normal form $NF_\Pi$]
\label{def:nf}
$NF_\Pi$ is the total map from well-typed terms $e$ to canonical terms induced
by the following structure-recursive rewrites, each of which either preserves
$\effect_\Pi$ or is the identity: erase SQL aliases and derived-expression
display names; sort conjunction literals in $p$, dependency fields $F$,
projection lists, group keys $G$, and aggregate families $\mathcal A$ by their
stable role-qualified field IDs and canonical expressions; sort join operands
and equality predicates by their child digests; sort union operands by digest
and collapse an identical duplicate branch only when the child normal form
proves full-tuple distinctness, so $e\cup_\delta e\equiv e$ for that
set-valued $e$; sort
relation-level union operands before any enclosing operator; and map a stable
page suffix to its catalog-canonical spelling (entity-key or all-group-key
suffix).  $NF_\Pi(q_1)=NF_\Pi(q_2)$ iff the two canonical terms are syntactically
identical.  The artifact realizing $NF_\Pi$ is
\texttt{taskgate-query-normal-form-v3}, and it performs no outer-join,
arithmetic, window, subquery, or general SQL equivalence reasoning.  This
query-level normalizer is distinct from the Fact-set $\operatorname{normalize}$
of Property~(Normalization), which acts on the final ledgers.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Typed normal-form soundness]
\label{thm:nf-soundness}
For admissible $\Pi$, immutable snapshot map $D$, typed $q_1,q_2$, and the
same visible field set $V$,
\[
 NF_\Pi(q_1)=NF_\Pi(q_2)\Longrightarrow
 \effect_\Pi(q_1,D,V)=\effect_\Pi(q_2,D,V).
\]
This implication is not a completeness claim for arbitrary equivalent SQL.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof in the supplement.}

\begin{theorem}[Base-ledger split/page/join conservation]
\label{thm:base-conservation}
Merging release/dependency observations for disjoint projections or a complete
page partition equals observing the combined result. Overlapping pages and
repeated terminal observations charge each release/dependency FactID at most
once; join fanout changes witness multiplicity but not the union of dependency
support. Outcome facts remain per normalized proposition and intentionally do
not collapse a family of different page or threshold questions.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof in the supplement.}

V2 uses \code{taskgate-query-normal-form-v3}. Besides the rewrites above it
binds namespace, snapshot, lineage digest, canonical SQL types, collation
names/versions, PostgreSQL three-valued bag semantics, UTC, and exact numeric
mode. It deliberately performs no outer-join, arithmetic, window, subquery, or
general SQL equivalence reasoning.

\section{Online Accounting}
\label{sec:online}

Formal semantics and the independent oracle cover scan/select/project, group
with four aggregates, inner equijoin, union-distinct, and page. Online lowering
is narrower: one Catalog product, or the bounded two-input forms below, may feed
grouping; pagination is one-product only. PostgreSQL end-to-end tests execute
paired statements and derive V2 release/dependency observations for hidden keys, join fanout,
complete union members, downstream aggregates, and page withholding. The
supplement gives the four-way coverage matrix. ``Online'' never means arbitrary
SQL containing the operator is accepted.

\subsection{Snapshot Publication and Streamed Derivation}

An exposure-enabled grant accepts either a one-product structured plan or one
closed two-input form.  The latter is (i) an inner join of two distinct
Catalog-stable roles with one or more same-type, same-collation equalities, or
(ii) union-distinct over two filtered aliases of one product, with the complete
deduplication schema explicit even when final $V$ hides some of its fields.
Nested join/set trees, self-join roles, union-all, and relational pagination
fail closed.  Callers cannot supply SQL fragments, FactIDs, provenance, or
semantic role identities.  Branch aliases are syntactic; join and union output
roles must match Catalog identities.

V4 first compiles each frozen Reporting Snapshot as an immutable publication.
The compiler calls the existing canonical FactID encoder, creates a separate
segment for base rows and for each base-cell field, sorts each segment by the
full 256-bit FactHash, and assigns a 32-bit ordinal. A segment is split by a
deterministic hash prefix before reaching $2^{32}$ entries. A Business-PG
sidecar maps each attested entity key to a dense row handle and the applicable
row/cell ordinals. The hot artifact contains only this map, ordinal-to-hash
arrays, bounds, and digests; canonical payloads remain in a sealed,
content-addressed cold artifact. Control storage may split and compress that
artifact into verified chunks. The publication manifest binds the datasource,
schema, snapshot, sidecar, dictionaries, compiler/profile versions, type and
collation contracts, and every artifact digest. Duplicate entity keys,
hash/payload collisions, count mismatches, or non-canonical artifacts prevent
publication.

The online compiler still validates fields, predicates, grouping, and one of
four aggregates; \code{COUNT(*)} and \code{COUNT(field)} retain distinct normal
forms. It emits a visible statement and an ordinal companion. A join companion
streams each matched handle pair and the fields needed by projection, join,
filter, group, and aggregate rules. A union-distinct companion uses
\code{UNION ALL} plus a branch marker so that every member of a delivered class
remains reconstructible. Both statements pass the PostgreSQL AST policy and run
in one explicit read-only \code{REPEATABLE READ} transaction
\cite{postgres16transaction}. The small visible result is buffered; the
companion is ordered by canonical output group and streamed.

The ordinal engine retains only the current group's support bitmap, an
ordinal-to-extra-multiplicity sparse map, and query-level dependency bitmaps.
Same-proof witnesses add multiplicities and alternative proofs take their
pointwise maximum exactly as in the algebra. At a group boundary, a k-way merge
over hash-sorted segments produces the same witness commitment and derived
release FactID as Eq.~\eqref{eq:observe}; those sparse derived releases and the
outcome fact enter dynamic dictionaries. Release is therefore a union of
base-cell and derived-release bitmaps, dependency uses base-row/base-cell
bitmaps, and outcome uses a dynamic bitmap. Unknown handles, out-of-range
ordinals, manifest mismatch, non-canonical bitmaps, overflow, or companion
truncation fail closed before release. The V2/V3 materialized algebra remains
the independent test oracle, not the V4 hot path.

For segment $j$, let $D_j$ be the publication-time bijection from its canonical
FactIDs to ordinals and let $\operatorname{Decode}_j(A)=
\{D_j^{-1}(x):x\in A\}$. Dynamic segments use the same interface after an exact
hash/payload collision check. V4 uses portable segmented Roaring bitmaps
\cite{chambi2016roaring}; compression changes representation, not membership.

\begin{theorem}[Exact bitmap refinement]
\label{thm:bitmap-refinement}
For canonical bitmaps $A$ and $B$ in one dictionary segment,
$\operatorname{Decode}(A\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}B)=
\operatorname{Decode}(A)\cup\operatorname{Decode}(B)$,
$\operatorname{Decode}(A\mathbin{\mathrm{ANDNOT}}B)=
\operatorname{Decode}(A)\setminus\operatorname{Decode}(B)$, and
$\operatorname{popcount}(A)=|\operatorname{Decode}(A)|$. For every successful
query in the closed online algebra, decoding all streamed bitmap components
yields exactly $\effect(q,D_s)$.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Intuition.} Bijective decoding turns OR, ANDNOT, and popcount into exact
set operations; induction over the closed algebra then substitutes ordinals
for singleton facts without changing support or witness commitments. The full
proof is in the supplement.

\subsection{Atomic Settlement}

For one successful V4 query, the gateway performs the following steps
(Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture-settlement}b):

\begin{enumerate}
\item Reauthorize the fixed plan and complete the ordinary resource reservation.
Lookup of a semantic replay entry occurs only after binding the signed grant and
publication.
\item On a miss, execute and buffer the visible statement while streaming its
ordinal companion in the same Business-PG snapshot, then derive the exact
three-dimensional bitmap observation. On a hit, load only an already committed
observation reference and encrypted materialization.
\item In one Control-PG transaction, read the root head and epoch. For each
dimension compute $E_d\mathbin{\mathrm{ANDNOT}}K_d$ and its popcount, reject if
$|K_d\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}E_d|>B_d^{Catalog}$, and create immutable copy-on-write
containers keyed by dictionary, segment, and high 16 ordinal bits.
\item Publish the Release, dependency, and Outcome set manifests through one
root-head epoch compare-and-swap. On conflict, reread the head and recompute all
three dimensions; they are never committed independently.
\item In that same transaction, settle query/row/database-time resources,
store the encrypted result and committed observation, append terminal audit
evidence, and persist a signed V6 receipt.
\item Commit and only then return the buffered or freshly re-encrypted result.
\end{enumerate}

Content-addressed containers and manifests are immutable; only the root head is
mutable. Any budget failure, corrupt dictionary, CAS exhaustion, or persistence
error rolls back containers, head, result, observation, audit, cache entry, and
receipt. Saving $(\code{root_task_id},\code{observation_digest})$ is also exact:
because a successfully committed root ledger grows monotonically, a repeated
committed observation has zero novelty without rescanning its bitmap.

Semantic replay is distinct from same-request idempotence. Its key binds the
task and grant/scope digest, typed normal form and every parameter, Catalog,
schema, publication and dictionary-set digests, and profile/compiler/order/page/
result-encoding versions. A hit skips Business and provenance SQL but still
reauthorizes, charges ordinary query/row resources, writes audit and a new
receipt, and re-encrypts under the new query's AAD. Cross-grant mismatch, cache
miss, erased key, removed result, or failed digest verification returns to the
novel path; the gateway never guesses a hit.

Activating $P_{e+1}$ changes the Catalog, publication, and dictionary-set
digests, so its semantic replay identity cannot equal a $P_e$ identity. New
tasks therefore miss the old cache by construction; entries for $P_e$ remain
usable only by roots still bound to $P_e$. No separate cache-invalidation
protocol is required for publication separation.

Let $F$ be the number of base facts in a publication, $n$ the streamed ordinal
references for a query, $s$ the number of touched segments, $c$ the number of
compressed containers touched during settlement, $g$ the largest one-group
support, and $x$ the number of multiplicity entries above one. Publication
construction sorts facts in $O(F\log F)$ comparison work (external sorting may
bound memory) and stores $32F$ hash bytes plus sidecar, compressed bitmap, and
cold-payload artifacts. Online derivation scans $n$ references; canonical
witness output uses $O(n\log s)$ merge work and $O(s)$ merge state. Its live
semantic state is $O(g+x)$ plus compressed query bitmaps rather than $O(n)$ Go
string/map/JSON objects. Novelty and union cost are proportional to the $c$
touched compressed containers, not to one SQL insert per fact. A verified
semantic replay makes exposure lookup constant-size metadata work, although
authorization, result re-encryption, audit, and ordinary resource accounting
still scale with their own payloads. These are design bounds, not V4 latency
measurements.

\subsection{Receipts and Failure Semantics}

A V6 Ed25519 receipt binds the root task, profile, Catalog SHA, dictionary set,
three effect digests, actual and charged counts, committed root epoch, result
digest, and SHA-256 of the normalized observation, in addition to authorization,
datasource, resource budget, and audit evidence. The dictionary set commits the
publication manifests. The receipt does not expose raw values or FactIDs.
Legacy receipt verifiers remain available, but V6 uses a disjoint digest domain.

Failures before connector invocation release both reservations. Ambiguous
connector outcomes conservatively charge the ordinary resource reservation but
release exposure because no result can be delivered before successful
settlement. A process restart applies the same rule. Terminal request replay is
observational: it returns the first persisted status or result and never starts
a second execution.

Withholding the buffer does not hide the decision bit. Whether settlement
accepts a query reveals whether its data-dependent novel Effect fits the
remaining budgets, and a rejected Effect is not committed to any ledger
dimension. Thus rejection can act as an uncharged threshold oracle. The current
protocol is not a simulatable auditor and makes no denial-noninterference claim.

\section{Delegation}
\label{sec:delegation}

\subsection{Root-Family Conservation}

A child authorization signs both \code{root_task_id} and
\code{parent_task_id}. Its human subject, catalog, datasource, schema, and
profile remain fixed, while products, columns, scopes, sensitivity, expiry, and
all budget dimensions may only narrow. Every execution revalidates the signed
parent grant and active ancestor chain.

All descendants resolve to one root ledger. If a parent and child request the
same fact, only the first successful settlement accounts it. Revoking the root
blocks new descendant queries, while an already terminal request ID remains
readable. Consequently, creating workers or task nodes cannot create fresh
exposure capacity. A child that signs a lower exposure ceiling may add novel
facts only while the shared family total remains within that ceiling; a
zero-novelty replay remains admissible.

This conservation scope stops at the root boundary. A second root task for the
same principal has an independent ledger and therefore a fresh budget if a
human approves it. The prototype has no principal-, tenant-, or organization-
wide exposure ledger, so neither delegation monotonicity nor the theorem below
prevents cross-root accumulation.

The boundary also stops at a publication epoch. The ledger subject is the pair
$(T,P_e)$, not a permanent model of the user's knowledge. A new $P_{e+1}$ is
available only to a newly approved root with a new ledger; an existing root
continues against $P_e$ through a retained version route (or fails closed if
that route is unavailable), and its ledger is neither copied nor migrated. Because
snapshot identity is part of FactID identity, unchanged business content may
be accounted again after independent approval in $P_{e+1}$. This conservative
choice deliberately avoids cross-publication deduplication. Such accounting
would require a principal- or tenant-wide semantic identity and policy, outside
the task-scoped guarantee.

\begin{theorem}[Atomic root-family budget safety]
\label{thm:root-family-safety}
For each $d\in\{r,i,o\}$, after any committed settlement sequence the decoded
root head satisfies
\[
 K_d=\bigcup_{q\in\mathrm{settled}(T)}E_d(q),\qquad |K_d|\le B_d^{Catalog}.
\]
This statement is independent of whether $K_d$ is materialized as FactID rows
or represented by immutable dictionary bitmaps.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Intuition.} Every descendant shares one root head; exact difference and
one three-dimensional CAS linearize $K_d\cup E_d$, while every bound is checked
before publication and conflicts recompute from the fresh head. The full proof
is in the supplement.

\textbf{Catalog-issued capacity.} The three ceilings $B^r_T,B^i_T,B^o_T$ are
human-governed capacity declarations, not learned risk scores or optimization
targets. An organization publishes a versioned profile with its data product
and sensitivity policy; the approval manifest binds the complete profile to the
root task. Normal issuance grants that full approved capacity. TaskGate assigns
no value to unused capacity, does not infer a smaller allowance from Agent
behavior, and does not optimize budget utilization. An explicitly narrower
human grant remains an authorization decision, not an automatic calibration
step. Hence 1,000 means capacity for 1,000 distinct semantic facts, not
``risk 1,000,'' and the profile must remain acceptable when all 1,000 units are
consumed. Query count, row, and DB-time limits are separate availability guards.

Unit weights provide representation-independent integers, exact replay/delegation
set union, and stable audit thresholds. They do not equate harm: field and
sensitivity policy remains the first boundary. A harm model may maintain a
separate sensitivity-weighted ledger over the same FactIDs; neither that model
nor differential-privacy composition is claimed here.

\section{System Realization}
\label{sec:system}

The prototype is implemented in Go with separate Control and Business
PostgreSQL 16 databases. A V4 Catalog declares reporting views, mandatory
scopes, immutable snapshot publications, entity keys, allowed operations,
approval routes, and resource/exposure profiles. Each Product references a
publication that binds its source, namespace, schema, snapshot, ordinal
sidecar, dictionaries, and manifest digest. Startup verifies the entire hot
artifact before activation; per-query attestation rebinds datasource identity,
database, role, PostgreSQL version, ordered view schema, and publication.
One service instance is Catalog-bound and rejects a task/Catalog mismatch.
During a publication rollover, a deployment therefore version-routes old roots
to retained $P_e$ instances while a $P_{e+1}$ instance accepts new
approvals; rollover never changes an active task in place.

Clients use MCP tools to list products, request a task, execute one structured
plan, inspect both budgets, delegate a narrower task, and retrieve results or
receipts. An external approval adapter
signs the immutable grant. Arbitrary direct SQL remains available only for
legacy resource-only grants; it fails closed when exposure evidence is
required.
The wire and storage label \code{influence} is retained for compatibility;
under the V2/V3 profiles it denotes positive-output dependency, not causal
influence.

The Control schema stores immutable dictionary manifests and chunks, sparse
dynamic release/outcome dictionaries, content-addressed bitmap containers and
set manifests, one three-dimensional head/epoch per root, committed
observations, and constant-size query references. Canonical payload blocks are
cold and expanded only for audit; they do not occupy the Gateway's resident hot
heap. Result rows are encrypted with AES-GCM and audit events form a hash chain.
A single high-cardinality semaphore isolates million-fact derivation from the
small-query pool; a query-private encrypted spool is used once the configured
working-set threshold is crossed.

Publication retention follows active task references. The current publication
and every older publication with a live root retain their complete HOT, COLD,
sidecar, and dictionary artifacts locally. Once the last bound root terminates,
COLD payloads may move to audit object storage, while manifests, digests,
receipts, and audit-chain evidence remain for the policy-defined audit period.
Local artifacts and replay entries may be reclaimed only when no live root
references the epoch and its retention deadline has expired. With root
lifetimes bounded by one daily refresh interval, this normally requires the
current and immediately preceding publications; longer tasks extend the set.
The prototype does not evaluate archival bandwidth or automated garbage
collection.

V4 is a clean-cut migration. The process refuses activation when a legacy
per-Fact ledger is non-empty and requires an explicit offline rebuild; it does
not silently delete history, dual-write, or infer ordinal membership from an
incomplete ledger. The old materialized algebra and receipt verifiers remain
test/compatibility paths, not V4 production storage.

\section{Finite-State Analysis}
\label{sec:formal}

The split TLA+ model \code{ExposureLedger.tla} contains one root, one child, two
requests, two facts in each ledger universe, all three limits set to one, and one
terminal replay. Its transitions are reserve, execute/buffer, derive,
settle, over-budget reject, pre-execution release, replay, and root revocation.
It checks three-budget safety, exact novel charge, no delivery before settlement,
no charge without settlement, buffered rejection, evidence/buffer agreement,
non-overlapping novel charges, task-family non-amplification, and at-most-once
physical execution for delivered requests.

Using pinned TLC 1.7.1, the provenance-checked run generated
\ExposureFormalStates{} states, found \ExposureFormalDistinct{} distinct states,
and reached depth \ExposureFormalDepth{} with no invariant error. Model,
configuration, and raw-log digests are validated before this manuscript builds
\cite{lamport2002specifying}.

This is exhaustive only for the submitted finite configuration. The model
assumes exact fact derivation, abstracts SQL bytes and cryptographic hashes, and
does not mechanically prove that Go/PostgreSQL refines TLA+. The repository
therefore includes an action-to-method-to-transaction-to-test refinement map
rather than claiming a proof that does not exist.

The finite \code{ExposureBitmapRefinement.tla} model separately represents an
immutable ordinal--FactID bijection, exact OR/ANDNOT/popcount, stale-epoch
refresh, and one three-dimensional head commit. Its configured invariants
require decoded bitmap state to equal the abstract FactSet state after every
transition and a committed-observation replay to add no facts. Its independently
digest-checked TLC run generated \BitmapFormalStates{} states, found
\BitmapFormalDistinct{} distinct states, and reached depth
\BitmapFormalDepth{} without an invariant error. This bounded refinement
obligation is not a proof for unbounded dictionaries or the Go/Roaring
implementation.

\subsection{RQ5: Daily Publication Refresh}

\textbf{Question.} Can TaskGate refresh a daily Reporting Snapshot while
preserving task binding, root-ledger isolation, and replay separation? We use
four deterministic, TPC-H-shaped (not official TPC-H) publications of
\RQFiveRows{} rows and \RQFiveFacts{} FactIDs each. Day~0 is the base; Days~1
and 2 modify the first 1\% and 5\% of sorted rows; Day~3 modifies 10\% and
inserts/deletes 1\%. Each day has an excluded calibration followed by
\RQFiveBuildRunsPerDay{} measured production build--strict-verify--receipt-bound
activate sequences. Their direct-child wall intervals and builder VmHWM exclude
Compose startup, PostgreSQL memory, and cache reset. The gate is five minutes
per summed sequence for this daily dataset, not an industry SLO.

\textbf{Correctness conditions.} After $P_{e+1}$ activates: (1) a live root
bound to $P_e$ returns $P_e$ data through its version-routed service; (2) a new
root resolves and observes $P_{e+1}$; (3) the old root head and ledger digests
are unchanged by activation and $P_{e+1}$ traffic; (4) a $P_{e+1}$ request
cannot hit a $P_e$ replay entry; and
(5) every child retains its root's publication. Publication, Catalog,
dictionary-set, direct-oracle result, cache, and ledger digests check these
conditions; result equality alone is insufficient.

\textbf{Results.} All \RQFiveTotalBuildRuns{} measured sequences pass. Maximum
build, verification, activation, and summed times are \RQFiveMaxBuildS{},
\RQFiveMaxVerifyS{}, \RQFiveMaxActivateS{}, and \RQFiveMaxCycleS{}~s;
maximum direct-builder VmHWM is \RQFiveBuilderPeakGiB{}~GiB. Each epoch is
about \RQFiveArtifactGiB{}~GiB total and \RQFiveHotArtifactMiB{}~MiB HOT. A
separate experiment-only pointer routes approvals and task IDs over four
retained Catalog-bound Services and snapshot databases. Across
\RQFiveTransitions{} switches, pointer switch p50/p95 is
\RQFiveSwitchMedianMS{}/\RQFiveSwitchTailMS{}~ms, first-query p50/p95 is
\RQFiveFirstQueryMedianMS{}/\RQFiveFirstQueryTailMS{}~ms, and semantic-replay
p50/p95 is \RQFiveReplayMedianMS{}/\RQFiveReplayTailMS{}~ms. All
\RQFiveChecksPassed{}/\RQFiveChecksTotal{} conditions pass at every switch.
The online run regenerates the same row fingerprints but re-attests the live
views, so its artifacts are independently bound rather than asserted byte-equal
to the offline timing artifacts.

\subsection{Reproducibility Boundary}

RQ1--RQ3 use small fixtures. Legacy and V4 evidence is warm, single-host, and
TPC-H-derived (not official), excluding cold-cache, multi-node, and
production-SLO claims; V4 uses one index build and one deployment, not
\RQFourVFourRoots{} independent deployments. Retained raw data reconstruct
every digest gate. A later failure-only finalization fix has targeted and fresh
contention tests but does not relabel the archived V4 SLO. The supplement
records scope and exclusions. RQ5's cycle sums three
sequential warm child intervals; its router is an experiment controller, not a
measured production load balancer.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{Semantic scope.} V3 accounts each successful normalized proposition,
including an empty relation or zero scalar, but assigns one unit rather than
estimating its information content. It does not enumerate rejected rows as
dependencies or account non-output groups/pages, order/rank-only inputs,
timing, correlations, background knowledge, or physical reads. A single rich
proposition may reveal more than one bit. The mechanism is neither minimal
causal provenance nor differential privacy.

\textbf{Decision and subject scope.} Refusal exposes an uncharged threshold bit;
no denial noninterference is claimed. Ledgers cover one root family, so repeated
approval creates fresh capacity unless an external principal/tenant policy
prevents it. The same applies across independently approved publication
epochs; TaskGate does not model or deduplicate a principal's durable knowledge.

\textbf{Query and identity scope.} V4 SLO scope is a genuinely frozen,
manifest-proven publication, not a mutable source or an arbitrary MVCC view.
TaskGate targets immutable reporting publications produced by scheduled daily
synchronization. It does not query mutable OLTP primaries, serve continuously
changing CDC streams, or migrate an active root between publication epochs.
Minute- or second-level freshness would require an incremental-publication
design and a separate cross-epoch accounting argument.
Online support is one product or a bounded
two-role inner equijoin/one-product union-distinct, optionally grouped; nested
trees, self/outer joins, union-all, relational pagination, windows, negation,
recursion, and arbitrary subqueries fail closed. Stable entity keys and snapshot
publications are administrator declarations backed by compiler checks;
production still requires a trustworthy versioned publication pipeline and
collision review. The online gates exclude offline index build, arbitrary SQL,
mutable sources, and queueing delay.

\textbf{Cost and deployment.} The measured legacy per-Fact path is expensive:
the million-fact result is
\RQFourScaleHighNovelS{}/\RQFourScaleHighReplayS{}~s novel/replay and
\RQFourScaleGatewayGiB{}~GiB gateway memory. V4 removes that hot-path
representation; in the measured campaign its maximum-point novel/replay
p50 is \RQFourVFourNovelMedianS{}~s/\RQFourVFourReplayMedianMS{}~ms and its cgroup
peak is \RQFourVFourGatewayPeakMiB{}~MiB. Immutable indexes shift rather than
erase cost: only \RQFourVFourBuildRuns{} build and one deployment were measured,
and dynamic dictionaries, same-root CAS contention, retention automation, encrypted
spooling, and audit expansion still require production evaluation. The single
gateway, local tokens, demo approval service, and one connector do not establish
distributed, production, multi-engine, or live-agent behavior.

\section{Conclusion}

\system conserves typed release, dependency, and outcome exposure within one
approved root family and immutable Reporting Snapshot. Approval-time
publication binding stabilizes FactIDs, replay, delegation, and exact
withhold-before-settlement accounting; a later daily publication is reserved
for new roots. V4 replaces the legacy million-object hot path with immutable
ordinals, streamed bitmaps, sparse dynamic facts, and one three-dimensional
CAS. The formal models and scoped campaigns support exactness, atomicity, and
compact representation on one fixed host; RQ5 additionally supports daily
rollover within its stated measurement boundary. Arbitrary SQL, mutable
OLTP/CDC, cross-publication knowledge accounting, and multi-host latency remain
outside the claim.
